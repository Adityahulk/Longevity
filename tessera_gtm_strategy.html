Beachhead — first 15 paying clients, all founder-led
Month 1–3 · Hyderabad only · No paid ads · No marketing team · You are the entire funnel
12–15 paying clients
The Phase 1 rule: You are not running campaigns. You are having 60 conversations. Every client in Months 1–3 comes from direct, personal interaction. This is research disguised as sales — you learn which objection kills the deal, which language makes people say yes, and which type of person converts fastest. That data shapes everything from Month 4 onward.
The product you sell in Phase 1: Mostly Performance (₹35k) — that's where your gross margin lives. Foundations (₹18k) is offered only when price is the explicit objection — and even then you tee up an upsell to Performance at Week 4. Do NOT lead with Foundations; it trains the market that Tessera is a budget product. Continuum (₹75k) sells itself to 1–2 of the first 15 who are obviously serious.

Channel 3 — Referral system (engineer it, don't hope)
The Day 90 referral ask
At Day 90, every successful graduate is in the highest trust-and-enthusiasm state they will ever be in with you. This is your referral window. Engineer it, don't leave it to chance.
The Day 90 ask, exact words: "You've dropped 4.7 biological years in 90 days. Who in your network would actually do something with that information if you showed it to them? Can you introduce me to 2 people?"
Incentive structure: ₹3,000 account credit per referred client who enrolls (applies to Continuum maintenance or next-cycle retest panels — keeps them in your ecosystem). Or a free upgrade panel (costs you ₹1.5k, perceived value ₹5k).
Make sharing trivial: Give them a one-page anonymized before/after PDF they can forward. Give them the line: "I just finished a 90-day biological age program — my numbers actually moved. Here's who did it."

The funnel logic — Foundations is bait, not a destination
Why Foundations exists at ₹18k
Price-objection killer: when a prospect says "₹35k is too much," Foundations lets you keep them in the system instead of losing them. ~70% of Foundations buyers upgrade to Performance at Week 4 once they've seen their Day-1 panel and want the MD consult + deeper protocol.
Built-in upsell trigger: Foundations Day-1 report contains 1–2 "would-need-deeper-panel" markers explicitly flagged. Coach raises this at Week 4 check-in. Upsell line: "For ₹17,000 more we run the full 60-marker panel and add the MD consult — given what we saw on Day 1, that's probably worth it." 70%+ say yes.
The trap to avoid: do NOT advertise Foundations on LinkedIn or paid ads. It cheapens the brand and trains the market to expect ₹18k. Foundations exists only as a price-objection response in a 1:1 sales conversation.
Why Continuum is the high-LTV anchor
Sells at Day 90: 20–25% of Performance graduates convert to Continuum for cycle 2. The pitch: "You've moved your biomarkers in 90 days. The next 6 months is about locking it in + going deeper on the markers that didn't fully move." Mathematically, this is the path to ₹70k+ LTV.
Anchors price perception: having Continuum visible at ₹75k makes Performance at ₹35k feel reasonable. Without Continuum on the page, Performance looks expensive standing alone.
Corporate sales lever: for C-suite cohort sales (Phase 3), Continuum is the SKU. Founders & CEOs buy 6-month over 90-day.
Post-graduation retention — the missing piece
What about clients who finish Performance and don't want full Continuum? Add a "Maintenance" annual plan around Month 5–6: ₹15,000 for the year — 1 retest panel, async coach quarterly, supplement subscription. Costs you ₹6k to fulfill, ~60% margin, ~50% of graduates take it. That's your true recurring revenue line and the second-cycle data flywheel. Not on the public pricing page in Phase 1 — offer it only to graduates at Day 90.
