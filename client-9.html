Three things you should know about this 5.7. First, there is a genuine definitional conflict: the American Diabetes Association counts 5.7 to 6.4% as prediabetes, while your lab's printed scale says under 5.7 is normal and prediabetes starts at 5.8. What low creatinine actually means. Creatinine is a waste product of muscle, so a low value usually reflects less muscle mass, not weak kidneys. There is no evidence of reduced filtration here. The honest limitation

What this panel could not tell us

A corporate checkup is built to screen organs, and yours did that well. It is not built to quantify cardiovascular or metabolic risk. Below is exactly what is missing, what each test would unlock, and, just as importantly, which analyses we could not run at all.

The six tests we would add, in priority order

1 · Ferritin + transferrin saturation

Unlocks: whether your borderline red cells are early iron depletion, and whether low iron is falsely inflating your HbA1c.
Blocked without it: we cannot interpret your HbA1c 5.7% or your haemoglobin 13.0 with confidence. Two open findings, one test.

2 · ApoB and Lp(a)

Unlocks: your true atherogenic particle count, and whether you carry an inherited lipoprotein risk. Lp(a) is measured once in a lifetime.
Blocked without it: we cannot quantify your real cardiovascular risk, only estimate it from cholesterol. It also leaves the lipid-panel discrepancy unresolved.

3 · hs-CRP

Unlocks: your systemic inflammation level, a genuine independent cardiac risk factor.
Blocked without it: your biological age cannot be calculated at all, and we have no real read on inflammation. ESR 9.71 is too insensitive to substitute.

4 · Fasting insulin

Unlocks: HOMA-IR, the only way to see insulin resistance before glucose starts to move.
Blocked without it: your TG:HDL 3.34 stays a soft hint rather than a measured answer. We cannot tell you how hard your pancreas is working.

5 · MMA, homocysteine, folate

Unlocks: whether your indeterminate active B12 (45) is a genuine functional shortfall, plus an independent cardiac risk marker.
Blocked without it: we cannot say if your B12 needs treating, and we cannot assess your methylation status at all.

6 · Urine ACR, cystatin C, uric acid

Unlocks: early kidney protein leak, true filtration separated from muscle mass, and a metabolic marker this package omitted.
Blocked without it: the dipstick used here cannot exclude early kidney damage, and we cannot tell whether your low creatinine is muscle mass or something else.

Lever 01 · Nutrition Lead

Bring the particle count down

Diet realistically moves LDL and non-HDL by 10 to 20% before any medication, and your good triglycerides mean we can focus narrowly on fat quality and fibre.

  • Soluble fibre, 10 to 15 g a day: oats, barley, rajma and other legumes, chana, apples, flax, psyllium. This is the fraction that actually lowers LDL.
  • Swap saturated for unsaturated fat: less ghee, coconut oil, fried and outside food; more nuts, seeds, olive or mustard oil, and oily fish if you eat it.
  • Protein at every meal to support muscle, given the low creatinine, and to steady post-meal glucose.
  • Keep alcohol low, which protects the HDL and the liver numbers you currently have in good shape.
Provisional until we know your typical meals, whether you are vegetarian (which also bears on the B12), your weight and waist, and your alcohol intake.
Lever 02 · Training

Build muscle, raise HDL

Exercise is the one lever that improves your cholesterol ratio, your metabolic signal and your lean mass at the same time.

  • Resistance training, 2 to 3 sessions a week. Directly addresses the low creatinine and lean-mass signal, and improves insulin sensitivity.
  • Zone-2 cardio, 150 minutes a week at a pace where you can still hold a conversation. The most reliable way to raise a low HDL.
  • A short walk after meals, which blunts the post-meal glucose rises your eAG of 117 hints at.
Provisional until we know your current activity, time available and any injuries.
