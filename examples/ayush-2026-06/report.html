The strategy

Three pillars, not a checklist of fifteen fixes

Attack the roots and the leaves resolve. Everything in your plan ladders up to one of these three.

Pillar 1 · Cardiovascular

Lower ApoB, lift HDL, to offset the inherited Lp(a)

You can't change Lp(a), it's genetic and set for life. What you can move is ApoB, the particle count that actually does the damage. Get it down meaningfully and you offset the inherited risk. Your diet is already clean, so the levers that remain are the ones you're not yet pulling.

The moves: Zone-2 cardio (your own goal) · soluble fibre to 30-40g via oats, chia, psyllium and dal, not vegetables you won't eat · swap saturated fat for unsaturated (less ghee, fewer yolks, lower-fat milk) · algal omega-3 · cut the occasional smoking/vaping entirely · physician chat on early ApoB-lowering.
Target by Day 90: ApoB 96 → under 85 · LDL 135 → under 115 · HDL 43 → 48+ · non-HDL 152 → under 135
Pillar 2 · Iron

Domain 01 · Training Lead

Move the way your goal needs

Right now you do roughly 20 minutes of steady cardio a week while training hard every day. That single gap is why "better cardio" is your goal and why your HDL is low-ish. Fixing it serves your goal, your lipids and your insulin signal at once. The plan is to redistribute your load, not add to it.

Weekly structure

  • Zone-2 cardio: 150 min/week, in 3-4 sessions of 35-45 min at a conversational pace (heart rate roughly 120-140). Cycle, incline treadmill, or aerobic shadow-boxing rounds. During Noida's high-AQI months (Oct-Feb), keep these indoors with filtered air, it matters for your nose and your heart.
  • HIIT: 1 session/week (your Saturday Monster-Zero slot). Down from near-daily, the extra intensity is feeding under-recovery and possibly worsening iron loss through training.
  • Resistance: 4 sessions/week. Keep your trainer-guided push/pull compound work, it's clearly working. Protect the knees: no explosive or plyometric knee loading.
  • MMA: 3-4 sessions/week (down from daily).
  • One full rest or active-recovery day. Walk and mobility only. Non-negotiable, this is the recovery pillar in action.
  • Daily 10-min mobility, knee-led.

The 12-week progression

  • Weeks 1-2, redistribute: add 2 Zone-2 sessions, claim Sunday as full rest. Add no intensity.
  • Weeks 3-6, build the base: 3 Zone-2/week, trim HIIT to one. Watch your resting heart rate start to drop and "cardio" start to feel different.
  • Weeks 7-10, progress + first deload: 4 Zone-2 on lighter weeks. Week 8 is a deload (volume down ~40%). Overload resumes week 9.
  • Weeks 11-13, consolidate: hold the structure. No heavy training in the 48 hours before your Day-90 retest, hard sessions can skew the blood panel.

Evidence: Zone-2 for metabolic and lipid health [E1]; resistance training maintenance [E1]; deload for recovery [E2].

Domain 02 · Recovery & Sleep Lead

The cheapest win in this whole report

You sleep about six hours, wake "always groggy," snore on your back, and feel perpetually tired while training daily. Short sleep alone lifts insulin resistance, blunts recovery and flattens mood, it sits under three of your findings. Moving bedtime from 10:30 to 9:45 buys you 45-60 minutes for free, while protecting your 5:45 wake.

  • Bed by 9:45, screens off by 9:15. You sometimes use screens in bed, this is the one to break. Magnesium glycinate (you already take it) at 9:00. Your room is already perfect: dark, 19°C.
  • Side-sleep, and see an ENT. You snore on your back and one side of your nose is always blocked. That nasal obstruction is very likely degrading your sleep quality, the real reason behind "always groggy." The ENT referral below ties your nose, your iron and your sleep together.
  • 10 minutes of daylight after waking. You get only 2-3 hours of daylight a day, mostly indoors. Morning light anchors your rhythm and softens both the groggy mornings and the afternoon crash.
  • No caffeine after 1pm. Specifically drop the after-lunch coffee, it protects your already-short sleep and your iron absorption in one move.
  • Beat the afternoon crash with a 10-min walk after lunch, not a recline. It blunts the post-rice glucose dip that's half the reason you crash.

Evidence: sleep extension for insulin sensitivity and recovery [E1]; morning light for circadian alignment [E2].

Domain 03 · Nutrition

Two surgical changes, not an overhaul

Your diet is genuinely excellent and we're protecting it. We change exactly two things: add soluble fibre without using vegetables you won't eat, and shift fat quality while adding heme iron. Everything is built to survive travel, your stated biggest obstacle.

Daily targets

  • Protein ~140g (you're already there) · fibre 35g, of which 8-10g soluble · keep the Tue/Thu fasts, they're a good habit.

Fibre, the no-vegetable way

  • Psyllium husk (isabgol), 1 tsp to 1 tbsp in water daily. The single highest-leverage move for ApoB and LDL, and it tastes of nothing.
  • Oats (already daily) + 1 tbsp chia + 1 tbsp ground flax stirred in, soluble fibre plus plant omega-3.
  • Dal up from 2x to 4-5x/week, plant iron too, paired with vitamin C and kept away from coffee.
  • Berries (you like blueberry), guava, apple-with-skin, easy fibre with zero vegetables.

Fat quality + iron

  • Cut the leftover ghee, use groundnut/mustard/olive oil with a light hand.
  • On heavy-egg days, go 2 whole + 3 whites instead of 5 whole, same protein, less saturated load. Whites are unlimited.
  • Switch coffee/shake dairy to lower-fat lactose-free milk.
  • Reintroduce red meat (mutton) 1-2x/week, your best absorbable iron, you simply weren't eating it.
  • Vitamin C 500mg (you already own it) with the iron meal; move coffee and the calcium supplement 1-2 hours away.

How we'll measure it

The retest plan, so change is measured not assumed

  • Day 30: subjective, fatigue, morning grogginess and the Bevel stress score should already be moving as sleep extends and the rest day lands.
  • Day 60: a lipid recheck (ApoB, LDL, HDL, non-HDL), lipids turn over in about six weeks, plus iron studies if the physician has started repletion.
  • Day 90, full panel (same lab, same morning slot, 12h fasted). Priority remeasures: ferritin, transferrin saturation, ApoB, LDL, HDL, HOMA-IR, hsCRP, HbA1c. Add-ons worth doing: a one-time Lp(a) confirm, stool occult-blood, and a reticulocyte-Hb or soluble transferrin receptor to confirm the iron response.
  • Week 12: a Vitamin D recheck to confirm the stepped-down dose holds you at 40-60.

One honest expectation to set: your Lp(a) will not change, it's genetic. The win there is offsetting it through ApoB, not lowering the Lp(a) number itself. Everything else on this list should move.
