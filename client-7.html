Lever 01 · Nutrition Lead

Lower the atherogenic particle load

Your single biggest lever. Diet realistically moves LDL and ApoB by 10 to 20% and triglycerides by more, before any medication.

What to add

  • Soluble fiber, 10 to 15 g/day (of 30 to 40 g total): oats, barley, rajma and other legumes, chana, apple, flax, psyllium. This fraction is what actually pulls LDL and ApoB down.
  • Unsaturated fats in place of saturated: mustard, olive or groundnut oil, nuts, seeds, avocado. If not vegetarian, oily fish twice a week; if vegetarian, algae omega-3.
  • Plants at volume: 400 g+ vegetables and fruit daily for fiber, potassium and polyphenols that lower inflammation.

What to pull back

  • Saturated fat under ~7% of calories: ghee, coconut, palm oil, full-fat dairy, fried food. This is the main dietary driver of your ApoB.
  • Refined carbohydrate and sugar: white rice in large portions, maida, sweets, sugary drinks. These feed your triglycerides and TG:HDL.
  • Alcohol to a minimum: it lifts both triglycerides and liver enzymes, two things already flagged.

A sample day (vegetarian-leaning, to be re-fitted to your actual diet):

BreakfastVegetable oats or besan chilla, a handful of walnuts or soaked almonds, fruit
Lunch2 rotis or a fist of rice, dal or rajma, a large sabzi, salad, curd
SnackRoasted chana or fruit, green tea
DinnerPaneer or tofu or fish, vegetables, a small portion of grain, finished 3+ hours before bed
Provisional until we have your typical meals, vegetarian status, waist and alcohol intake. Those answers turn this template into your plan.
Lever 02 · Training

Zone-2 for the arteries, strength for the metabolism

Exercise is the one lever that raises HDL, lowers triglycerides, improves insulin sensitivity, supports testosterone and reverses fatty liver at the same time.

  • Zone-2 cardio, 150 to 180 min/week across 3 to 4 sessions. That is a brisk pace where you can just hold a conversation, roughly 60 to 70% of max heart rate (about 110 to 130 bpm for you, to be refined). Brisk walking, cycling or incline treadmill all work. This is the biggest single mover of HDL and triglycerides.
  • Strength training, 2 to 3 sessions/week: compound movements (squat, hinge, push, pull). Muscle is a glucose and triglyceride sink, and resistance work supports your low-normal testosterone.
  • One short interval session/week once a base is built, to lift cardiorespiratory fitness.
  • 8,000 to 10,000 steps/day as a floor of daily movement.
Provisional until we know your current activity, time available and any injuries. Note: avoid a hard session in the 2 to 3 days before any blood retest, so it does not falsely lift your liver enzymes again.
