US operations · end-to-end playbook

How we run a US member, step by step, from consult to retest.

In India we collect samples at home cheaply. In the US the economics invert, so the model inverts with it: we rent every physical rail — the lab network, the ordering physician, the phlebotomist, the genome lab, the wearable API — and keep only the record and the reasoning. This page is the exact operating procedure: the one-time stack you stand up once, then every step a single member moves through.

00 · The operating principle

Rent every rail. Own the record and the reasoning.

A US blood draw carries real labour and regulatory cost that a home draw in India does not: a phlebotomist's time, a physician's order, and CLIA-lab processing. The instinct is to build a collection operation. That is the wrong instinct. Every physical step in the US already exists as a rentable service, and each one has driven its price to near-cost fighting for the same customers.

So Antiaging Labs stays asset-light: we never touch a sample, never own a lab, never employ a phlebotomist. We integrate the existing rails and keep the two things that compound — the longitudinal record and the reasoning engine. The one hard layer to collect (blood) is also the one carrying our differentiated value; the two easy layers (genome, wearables) are where the member already has data to pull in.

India model

Home phlebotomy is cheap and ubiquitous → collect at the member's door. No dense walk-in network exists.

US model

Home phlebotomy is a $90–120 premium → default to the 2,000+ walk-in lab network; offer concierge draw as an upgrade.

What we never build

A lab, a phlebotomy fleet, an in-house ordering physician team, or a genome sequencer. All rented.

What we always own

The member record, the rulebook + engine, the protocol, the report, and the outcome dataset.

Positioning guardrail

The blood test is table stakes, not the product.

US direct-to-consumer panels have collapsed to $199–365/yr (Superpower $199, Function $365, 200k+ members). We do not compete on the panel — it rides along at cost like everyone else's. We price the program: the four-layer fusion, the root-cause reasoning, the daily agent and the retest loop. If a member is comparing us on "biomarkers per dollar," the fit call has failed.

N1 · Your first users, lean mode

Getting your first US user done tomorrow.

For the first handful of members, sign nothing. Every B2B rail below (§01) has minimums or MOQs that make no sense for one person. Instead you use consumer / direct-access rails + manual uploads — all of which the engine already parses. Your real out-of-pocket per user is roughly the cost of the LLM tokens; the member buys their own panel. Switch to the scaled stack around 20–50 users.

A · Blood — your custom 70+ panel, physician order included

Direct-access lab marketplaces (Ulta Lab Tests, Walk-In Lab, Labcorp OnDemand) include a network physician who signs the requisition — you never need your own doctor to order. Ulta also lets you build and save a custom panel template, so you assemble your Tessera 70+ marker list once and reorder it for every member.

  1. Set up once

    Create an Ulta Lab Tests (or Walk-In Lab) account. Build your Tessera 70+ custom panel — add each marker (CBC, CMP, lipids + ApoB + Lp(a), fasting insulin, HbA1c, hsCRP, homocysteine, thyroid, Vit D / B12 / folate, iron / ferritin, magnesium, testosterone, cortisol…) and save it as a reusable template.

  2. Check the member's state

    Direct-access works everywhere except a couple of restricted states (historically NY, NJ, RI). If the member is there, order through Labcorp OnDemand / Quest DTC instead.

  3. Order → get the requisition

    Place the order for the member. The marketplace's network physician signs it and you receive a printed requisition (PDF). No own doctor required.

  4. Collect — walk-in or at-home

    Walk-in (default): member takes the requisition to one of 2,100+ Quest / Labcorp centers, fasted 10–12 h, morning slot. Draw fee ~$10, 15-minute visit.

    At-home (premium): at Ulta checkout pick "Getlabs at-home (~$95)", or book at getlabs.com by uploading the requisition. A phlebotomist comes to home/office and delivers to the lab.

  5. Results → parse

    Values return in 24–72 h as a downloadable PDF. Run tessera-lab-parse → biomarkers.json.

All 70+ longevity markers are on the standard Quest/Labcorp menu the included physician can sign Only a truly exotic off-menu marker needs your own ordering physician (that's the §01 scaled path)

B · Genome — for that one user

  1. Path A · BYO upload (do this first — $0, instant)

    Ask if the member has 23andMe or AncestryDNA. If yes → Settings → Download Raw Data (a .txt) → they upload → tessera-genetics-ingest parses it immediately. ~30–50% of the ICP already has a file.

  2. Path B · Buy a consumer array kit ($99, no contract)

    If not, drop-ship an AncestryDNA or 23andMe kit to their address (~$99, often $39–59 on sale). A genotyping array is exactly what Deep needs. Member spits, mails prepaid → 3–6 wk → downloads raw file → uploads → ingest → imputation → PRS → genetics.json.

  3. Path C · WGS (only if the member wants max depth)

    Nucleus (~$300–400, 30x), Sequencing.com, or Dante Labs return a downloadable VCF you can ingest. Not needed for standard Deep.

Sequencing rule

Never let the genome block onboarding.

The genome is the slow layer (3–6 wk). Deliver the Essential protocol from blood + wearables + intake in ~a week, then fold the genome in when it lands to upgrade the member to Deep.

02 · One member, end to end

The nine operational steps.

Every US member moves through the same nine steps. Steps 3a–3d run in parallel — the slow genome layer never blocks the fast ones. The role that owns each step is tagged.

  1. Consultation & fit

    Member books the 30-minute consult (Calendly). We confirm they are a real fit — prevention-minded, willing to test, adhere and retest — and turn away poor fits honestly. Goal is set: reverse-aging or peak-performance (this only re-weights the protocol objective later; the pipeline is identical).

    Owner · Founder / Coach

  2. Tier selection & consent

    Member picks Essential (blood + intake + wearables), Deep (+ genome), or Comprehensive (+ imaging / clinical sequencing). Scoped, revocable consent is captured per data type: lab sharing, wearable import, genetic processing, research use, communication.

    Owner · Founder / Ops

  3. Kick off all four data layers at once

    The member portal fires four parallel tracks the same day: schedule blood, ship/collect genome, connect wearable, complete intake. Genome (3–6 wk) starts first precisely because it is slowest.

    Owner · Ops (automated)

  4. Blood drawn & results returned

    Physician order is generated via the telehealth rail, then the member either walks into a Quest/LabCorp (free, 15 min) or books a Getlabs home draw. CLIA lab returns values in ~2–3 business days; our normalizer parses them into biomarkers.json. (Detail in §03.)

    Owner · Lab Ops + engine

  5. Genome ingested

    BYO upload parses immediately; a branded kit returns a raw genotype file in 3–6 weeks. Either way it becomes genetics.json and upgrades the member from Essential to Deep when it lands. (Detail in §04.)

    Owner · engine

  6. Engine run

    The nine-agent pipeline runs: normalize → panel-completeness → intake → rule-evaluate → phenotype → root-cause → safety screen (hard gate) → protocol author → report. Every finding traces to a fired rule and a rulebook hash. (Detail in §06.)

    Owner · engine (deterministic)

  7. Human review

    A practitioner reviews the drafted protocol; a physician reviews any Tier-1 finding, pharmacogenomic advisory, or rare/actionable variant. Nothing reaches the member unreviewed.

    Owner · Practitioner + Medical Advisor

  8. Delivery & daily execution

    Member receives the report + protocol and begins the daily loop: the agent joins last night's wearable delta to its cause and the protocol's authorized response, emitting one honest summary + one doable action. (Detail in §07.)

    Owner · Coach + daily agent

  9. Retest & next cycle

    At the protocol's marker-specific interval (typically 10–12 weeks) the member retests the markers that were targeted. The engine computes deltas, attributes what moved, and authors the next cycle. This closes the loop and produces the outcome dataset.

    Owner · Lab Ops + engine + Coach
03 · The one hard layer

Blood: default walk-in, upsell concierge.

Blood is the only layer with labour, a physician order, and CLIA processing. Two collection paths; the member chooses by whether their time or their money is scarcer.

The two draw paths

Path A · Walk-in (default, free draw)

Member picks one of 2,000+ Quest or LabCorp sites, schedules online, 15-minute visit, 2–5 minute draw. Draw fee is bundled into the lab contract — no separate labour charge. Proven at scale: Function's 200k+ members use exactly this.

Path B · Concierge home draw (premium)

Getlabs sends a phlebotomist to home/office, ~$90–120. For the founder/HNI ICP whose time > $120, we bundle or discount this into Deep/Comprehensive and make it the norm — a differentiator Function/Superpower treat only as a paid add-on.

Operational sequence

  1. Generate the order

    Member's intake + goal produces the panel list (the 70+ longevity markers: ApoB, Lp(a), fasting insulin, hsCRP, homocysteine, etc.). The telehealth clinician reviews and signs the lab order. This is legally required in most states — never skipped.

  2. Route to a draw path

    Portal offers walk-in scheduling (Path A) or a Getlabs booking (Path B). Fasting guidance attached: 10–12 h fast, book an early-morning slot — the fast is the real friction, not the drive.

  3. Draw & lab processing

    Sample goes to Quest/LabCorp. Results return in ~2–3 business days to our portal via the Rupa/Vital rail.

  4. Normalize

    Our normalizer parses the values, converts units, reconciles lab-platform differences, computes derived indices (HOMA-IR, TG:HDL, ApoB:ApoA1, eGFR…) → biomarkers.json, with a panel-completeness flag for anything missing.

B2B unlock

Onsite draw days for tech companies.

For corporate cohorts, one phlebotomist draws 15–20 employees in a morning, amortizing the mobile fee to near zero per person. This is both a cheaper collection mode and a natural executive-health / corporate-wellness wedge — the same rail, batched.

Startup blood COGS via wholesale ≈ $100–250 per comprehensive panel, falling as volume earns better lab rates. We price the membership to absorb it.

04 · The easy layer

Genome: a saliva kit in the mail, or a free upload.

No phlebotomist, no physician order — consumer genomics rides the informational/wellness lane. The only real cost is turnaround, which we design off the critical path.

Path A · BYO upload ($0 to us)

~30–50% of the ICP already own a 23andMe/Ancestry raw file (both still downloadable in 2026 post-TTAM). They upload; tessera-genetics-ingest parses 23andMe/Ancestry/MyHeritage/Tellmegen formats instantly. Zero logistics, instant day-one "wow."

Path B · Branded saliva kit (Deep default)

We dropship a white-labeled genotyping kit (GeneMetrics / Nutrition Genome / Toolbox / Dante). Member spits, mails prepaid, CLIA lab runs the array in 2–4 wk, raw file returns to us. COGS ~$50–150, bundled into Deep.

Branded-kit sequence

  1. Ship

    Partner CLIA lab mails the branded saliva kit to the member's home.

  2. Collect & return

    Member provides a saliva sample, seals it, drops it in any mailbox with the prepaid return label. Total member effort: one spit.

  3. Lab runs the array

    Genotyping array (650k–1M SNPs — the same tech as consumer DNA, exactly what Deep needs). ~2–4 weeks.

  4. Ingest

    Raw genotype file → tessera-genetics-ingest → imputation (to ~10–40M variants) + PRS → genetics.json: risk, pharmacogenomic, methylation and nutrition/training groupings.

Array for Deep — cheap, sufficient WGS ($249–500) only for Comprehensive Turnaround 3–6 wk — kept off critical path

Sequencing matters more than speed. The genome is the slow layer, so onboarding never waits on it. We generate the Essential protocol from blood + wearables + intake immediately, then fold the genome in when it arrives to upgrade the member to Deep. And post-23andMe-bankruptcy, running our own kit with a clear "your genome, your control, deletable, never sold" policy is a trust differentiator, not just logistics.

05 · The zero-logistics layers

Wearables and intake: pure software.

Neither requires shipping anything. The ICP is the most wearable-saturated population on earth (~60–80% own one), so this is an API call and a form, not a collection problem.

Wearables

  1. Connect

    Member authorizes Vital or Terra (unified API across Oura / Whoop / Apple Watch / Garmin / Fitbit / Eight Sleep). Apple Health XML export is the manual fallback.

  2. Normalize

    tessera-wearable-ingest builds 7/30/90-day rolling means for HRV (RMSSD), RHR, sleep duration/efficiency/deep%/REM%, steps, VO₂max, training-load ACWR, and flags behaviour-correlated anomalies (z-score deviations tagged with same-day context).

  3. Feed the loop

    Trends label each metric optimal/watch/off and become confirmation edges for root-cause (HRV↓ confirms HPA activation, etc.) and the daily agent's live sensor stream.

Intake

tessera-intake-v2 runs the deep intake in the portal: identity/goals, body/medical, diet, exercise, sleep/stress, plus HPI/current-symptoms, family-history depth, constraints (budget, kitchen, cuisine, religious, schedule), environmental exposures (US AQI/occupation), and a mental-health micro-screen (PHQ-2/GAD-2/PSS-4). This is the context layer that stops biomarkers from being misread and makes the protocol buildable from constraints, not ideals. Emits intake.json.

06 · Reasoning, review, delivery

Four layers in, one reviewed protocol out.

Once the four artifacts exist, the deterministic engine runs and humans review before anything ships. This is the same pipeline documented on /engine; the US specifics are the review gates.

Engine

Nine-agent run

normalize → panel-completeness → intake → rule-evaluate → phenotype → root-cause → safety-screen (hard gate) → protocol-author → report. Same inputs + rulebook version = same protocol, with a hashed run-manifest.

Fusion

Convergence scoring

A finding escalates only when independent layers agree: 1 layer = watch, 2 = act, 3 = root cause. Genome modifies thresholds/weights, never fires an action alone.

Review 1

Practitioner

Reviews the drafted 5-domain protocol against the member's constraints and adherence capacity; edits for realism.

Review 2

Medical Advisor (when triggered)

Signs off Tier-1 findings, pharmacogenomic advisories (e.g. SLCO1B1 statin), and routes rare/actionable variants + positive mental-health screens to counseling/professional support.

Deliver

Report + protocol

Member receives the rule-traced report and the personalized protocol across Nutrition, Training, Recovery, Supplements, Mind — every recommendation safety-screened and sourced.

07 · The living loop

Daily execution, then recalibration.

Two clocks run after delivery. The heavy engine only re-runs on new blood (every 8–12 weeks). Between draws, a bounded daily agent keeps the protocol alive without re-deriving it.

Clock 2 — the daily agent

Each morning it pulls last night's wearable, recomputes trends and anomalies, finds the same-day behavioural cause, applies the member's genomic/biomarker modifiers, and emits one honest summary + one authorized action. Its action space is enumerated by the protocol — it can move training intensity, caffeine timing, recovery emphasis, meal timing; it cannot invent a supplement, diagnose, or change a medical target. That bound is what makes it safe and cheap.

Example daily line

Anomaly + personal reason + authorized patch.

"HRV 34ms, −29% vs your baseline. Last night's 4pm espresso is still clearing — you're a CYP1A2 slow metabolizer. Today: skip the AM espresso, 20-min Zone-2 walk in daylight, push the hard session to tomorrow."

No wearable app can write that line — it requires all four layers fused with provenance.

Clock 1 — retest & next cycle

  1. Retest the targeted markers

    At the protocol's marker-specific interval, the member re-draws only the markers that were being moved (ApoB, HOMA-IR, hsCRP, homocysteine, Vit-D…) — same walk-in/concierge paths as §03.

  2. Delta & attribution

    The engine diffs baseline vs follow-up, names what moved and what didn't, and attributes movement to the interventions and adherence.

  3. Refine

    Next-cycle protocol is authored from the delta. Each completed baseline→follow-up pair becomes a labeled (intervention → response) tuple, stratified by genotype × phenotype — the compounding outcome dataset.

09 · Guardrails

Compliance & safety, non-negotiable.

Physician order

Most US states require a signed order for blood tests. Every panel routes through the telehealth clinician + Medical Director. Never a self-ordered draw.

Non-diagnostic framing

This is health-optimization guidance, not diagnosis or disease management. Consumer genomics stays informational; clinical sequencing is the counseled exception.

HIPAA + BAAs

Identifiable health data on HIPAA-compliant infra, a signed BAA with every rail (Rupa/Vital, Getlabs, genome lab, wearable API). Data never sold; consent scoped and revocable; deletion honored.

Hard safety gate

Agent 07 vetoes any intervention conflicting with the member's meds, renal function, pregnancy, anticoagulation, or allergies before the protocol is authored.

Genetic counseling

APOE ε4 and rare/actionable variants are disclosed with counseling context and a physician conversation — never surfaced casually in-app.

Mental-health routing

Positive PHQ-2 / GAD-2 screens route the member to professional support, not to an algorithmic nudge.

Asset-light

We rent every physical rail and own only the record and the reasoning. No lab, no fleet, no sequencer.

Parallel, not serial

The four data layers collect concurrently; the slow genome never blocks the first protocol.

Reasoning is the product

The panel is commoditized table stakes. We price the fusion, the root-cause logic, the daily agent and the loop.

The logistics are solved. The reasoning is the moat.

Blood is a walk-in or a concierge draw; genome is a mailed spit or an upload; wearables and intake are software. Every physical step is rented — what we keep is the record and the engine.
