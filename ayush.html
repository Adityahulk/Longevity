Discuss with your physician

Lipoprotein(a) is elevated at 49 mg/dL, and it's genetic.

Lp(a) sits at 49.44 mg/dL against a threshold of 30. Lp(a) is roughly 90% inherited, set for life, and an independent driver of heart-attack and stroke risk that standard cholesterol tests miss entirely. It does not respond to diet or exercise. This is not a cause for alarm at 25, it is a reason to take your other lipid numbers (LDL 135, ApoB 96, non-HDL 152) more seriously than someone without it, because the risks stack. South Asians carry elevated Lp(a) at higher rates than most populations.

What this means practically: (1) Confirm Lp(a) once with a repeat test, it should be stable for life. (2) Ask your physician whether any first-degree relative had a heart attack or stroke before 55 (men) or 65 (women), Lp(a) clusters in families. (3) The lever you can move is ApoB. Getting your ApoB and LDL down meaningfully offsets the inherited Lp(a) risk. That is what the protocol will target.

The lifestyle questionnaire

Your bloodwork tells us what is happening. These questions tell us why, and they decide your protocol. They are deliberately thorough, weighted toward your three findings (cardiovascular, iron, early insulin signal), but covering the full picture so nothing is guessed. Answer as honestly and specifically as you can. Rough numbers are completely fine, and "don't know / not sure" is a valid answer to any of them. You don't have to answer in order, voice notes work too.

15 sections. The starred sections (★) matter most for your specific results, if you're short on time, do those first.

01 · Body & baseline

The physical starting point

Needed for BMI, waist-to-height and to anchor every lipid and insulin target.

  1. Height and current weight?
  2. Waist circumference (measured at the navel, with a tape if you have one)?
  3. Hip circumference (so we can get a waist-to-hip ratio)?
  4. Has your weight changed in the last 6–12 months? Up, down, or stable, and by how much?
  5. What's the most you've ever weighed, and the least as an adult?
  6. How do your clothes fit lately, looser, tighter, same?
  7. How would you describe your build, lean, average, athletic, carrying some midsection weight?
  8. Where do you tend to gain weight first, belly, face, all over?
02 · Family history ★

What runs in the family (top priority, given Lp(a))

Your inherited Lp(a) makes family cardiovascular history the single most informative thing you can tell us. Be as specific with ages as you can.

  1. Any heart attack, stroke, angioplasty/stent, bypass, or sudden cardiac death in a parent, grandparent, uncle/aunt or sibling? Who, and at what age?
  2. Anyone diagnosed with high cholesterol, or on cholesterol medication (statins, etc.)?
  3. Anyone with very high Lp(a), or unexplained early heart disease despite being fit/slim?
  4. Family history of diabetes or pre-diabetes, and on which side?
  5. Family history of high blood pressure?
  6. Family history of thyroid disease or autoimmune conditions?
  7. Any cancers in the family, and which type/age?
  8. Are your parents and grandparents alive? If yes, current age and general health. If not, age and cause of death?
  9. Your family's region/community of origin (some lipid and metabolic patterns cluster by ancestry)?
