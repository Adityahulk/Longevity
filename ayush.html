Discuss with your physician

Lipoprotein(a) is elevated at 49 mg/dL, and it's genetic.

Lp(a) sits at 49.44 mg/dL against a threshold of 30. Lp(a) is roughly 90% inherited, set for life, and an independent driver of heart-attack and stroke risk that standard cholesterol tests miss entirely. It does not respond to diet or exercise. This is not a cause for alarm at 25, it is a reason to take your other lipid numbers (LDL 135, ApoB 96, non-HDL 152) more seriously than someone without it, because the risks stack. Elevated Lp(a) is common and often under-tested across populations.

What this means practically: (1) Confirm Lp(a) once with a repeat test, it should be stable for life. (2) Ask your physician whether any first-degree relative had a heart attack or stroke before 55 (men) or 65 (women), Lp(a) clusters in families. (3) The lever you can move is ApoB. Getting your ApoB and LDL down meaningfully offsets the inherited Lp(a) risk. That is what the protocol will target.
