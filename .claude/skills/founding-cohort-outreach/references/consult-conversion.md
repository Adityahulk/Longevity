# Consult Conversion — turning booked calls into users

A booked consult is not the finish line; a **member** is. This is the highest-leverage and most
neglected part of the funnel — outreach that books 15 calls but converts 2 is a failure. Use this
when the founder is preparing for a call, asking how to handle a price/skeptic objection, or
figuring out how to follow up after a consult.

The tone of the call must match the brand: honest, rigorous, non-pushy. The founder is a credible
expert helping a smart person make a good decision — including, sometimes, telling them *not* to
buy the top tier (or to start with Essential). That honesty is what converts this audience; a hard
close repels them.

## Before the call (2-minute prep)

Pull what you know about the person from the tracker and anything they shared, and walk in with:
- **Their likely "why now"** — the post, the checkup, the milestone birthday, the recovery problem
  that got them on the call. Open on it.
- **Their probable tier fit** — a fitness-obsessed quantified-self person leans Deep (genome + live
  twin); a curious professional just starting leans Essential. Don't pre-decide, but have a read.
- **One proof asset cued up** — usually the sample report (/client-5); for a skeptic-engineer, the
  whitepaper (/whitepaper); for the science-minded, the engine page (/engine).

## A loose 30-minute structure (not a script to read)

The point is a real conversation, not a pitch deck. Roughly:
1. **Their world (10 min, mostly listening).** What are they optimizing, what have they tried, what
   does their current bloodwork/wearable picture look like, what frustrates them. The more they
   talk, the better you tailor — and people convince themselves as they articulate the problem.
2. **The reframe (5 min).** Connect their frustration to the wedge: their "normal" labs were read
   against the wrong population; their wearable sees symptoms not causes; nobody's reading DNA +
   labs + wearable *together* as one biological-age picture. This is the insight they came for.
3. **How it works, only as deep as they want (5-8 min).** The multi-omic engine, validated clocks
   (PhenoAge/KDM-BA), the traceable rule-based report, physician-in-the-loop, measure→intervene→
   re-test. Show the sample report. Stay honest about what's measured vs predicted.
4. **Tier fit + honest recommendation (5 min).** Recommend the *right* tier for them, including
   "start with Essential, add genome later" if that's genuinely better. Be clear that Essential is
   live today; Deep runs through a genome partner set up at onboarding; Comprehensive is referral.
5. **Clear next step (2 min).** One concrete action: which tier, what onboarding looks like, when
   the blood draw happens. Don't let it dissolve into "I'll think about it" without a defined
   follow-up date.

## Objection handling (honest answers, not rebuttals)

The job is to resolve the real concern truthfully, not to "overcome" it. If the honest answer is
"this isn't for you right now," give it — it protects the brand and your referrals.

- **"₹28K (or ₹65K) is a lot."** Reframe against what it replaces and what it is: a one-time
  diagnostic setup plus a year of an engine that tells you exactly what's aging you and what to do,
  read against South Asian baselines, re-tested to prove movement. Compare to the scattered, un-
  integrated spend they already make (random panels, supplements they don't need, a coach who can't
  see their bloodwork). Never discount on the spot out of panic; the founder-member framing is the
  lever, not price-cutting.
- **"I already get regular bloodwork."** Good — that's the raw material, not the answer. Bloodwork
  read against Western ranges, with no genome, no wearable, no biological-age model, and no
  re-test, is data without a decision. The product is the integration and the loop, not the panel.
- **"Is the genome / Deep tier actually worth it?"** Be honest: for some it changes the protocol
  (e.g. how they process nutrients, drugs, stress); for others Essential is plenty to start. Walk
  them to the right call rather than upselling. This honesty is disproportionately persuasive here.
- **"Does this even work / what are the results?"** Don't invent outcomes. The honest pitch is the
  *method*: validated clocks, transparent rule-traced reasoning, physician oversight, and a 90-day
  re-test that shows their own delta. You're selling a rigorous system and a measurement, not a
  promised number.
- **"I have [condition] / I'm on medication."** Reassure on the safety model (clinical filter,
  medical advisor, works alongside their doctor, never changes prescriptions) and be willing to say
  "let's get your physician in the loop first" for anything needing primary care.
- **"Let me think about it."** Usually an unspoken objection. Ask gently which part they're unsure
  on, resolve that, and set a specific follow-up date + the sample report to chew on.

## After the call

- **Same-day recap.** A short message: what you recommend for them, the price, the next step, and
  the proof asset. Specific and low-pressure. Update the tracker (`consult_done`, next_action).
- **One value-add follow-up** if they didn't commit, then a graceful close — same discipline as
  cold follow-ups (see playbook). Don't nag.
- **Always ask for a referral** from people who had a good call, whether or not they bought. Warm
  referrals from qualified people are your best remaining source for the next users.

## Why this matters for the goal

The whole skill exists to produce *members*, and the call is where booked interest becomes revenue
or evaporates. A founder who books 15 calls and converts 6-8 has the cohort; one who books 15 and
converts 1 will wrongly conclude the outreach failed when really the call did. Treat consult
conversion as part of the funnel, measure it (the tracker's `consult_done → member` step), and fix
it with honest preparation, not pressure.
