# Outreach Playbook

Per-channel tactics and templates for booking consults. Templates are **starting scaffolds, not
fill-in-the-blank forms** — the personalization is the product. If you can't make the first line
specific to the person, the message isn't ready. Adapt tone to the relationship: warmer and
looser for friends, tighter and more proof-led for strangers.

Every message obeys the brief: lead with their problem, one low-friction ask (the consult or
"want the details?"), short, honest, one link at most.

## Warm network / WhatsApp

Highest conversion, lowest scale. These people already trust the founder, so the goal is to make
it easy to say "sure, tell me more" without making it weird that a friend is now selling something.
Honesty and a personal "I'm building this, I want your eyes on it" beat a polished pitch.

**Direct ask (warm, fits the ICP):**
> Hey [name] — quick one. I've been building [Antiaging Labs], a longevity engine that pulls your
> DNA, bloodwork and wearable into one live read of how fast you're actually aging — calibrated to
> *South Asian* biology, not the Western ranges every Indian lab uses. I'm taking on a small
> founding group and thought of you because [you're always on top of your training / you mentioned
> that checkup thing]. Want me to send a 2-min overview, or easier to just grab 30 min?

**Referral ask (when they're not the user but know fits):**
> Hey [name] — building a precision-longevity thing for people who actually track their health
> ([antiaging-labs.com]). Not asking you to buy anything — but who's the most health-obsessed
> person you know in [Bangalore]? The one with the Whoop and the bloodwork spreadsheet. Would love
> an intro.

**Why this works:** names a real reason you thought of *them*, offers an even-smaller step than the
call ("send a 2-min overview"), and the referral version removes all pressure while mining their
network — often the single best source of first users.

## LinkedIn

The core channel for Indian metro professionals. Two motions: (1) post content so your profile is
warm before you DM, and (2) targeted connection + DM. People check who's messaging them, so the
founder's profile headline should already say what Antiaging Labs is.

**Connection note (≤300 chars, no pitch yet):**
> Hi [name] — came across your [post on sleep / profile]; we clearly nerd out on the same stuff. I'm
> building Antiaging Labs (precision longevity calibrated for South Asian biology). Would be good to
> connect.

**First DM after they accept (the actual ask):**
> Thanks for connecting, [name]. Quick context on why I reached out: most of us get bloodwork back,
> hear "everything's normal," and have no idea if we're actually aging fast or slow — and those
> "normal" ranges aren't even built for South Asian bodies. I'm building the thing that fixes that:
> one engine over your genome, labs and wearable that tracks your biological age and what to do
> about it.
>
> I'm onboarding a small founding group right now. Given how seriously you take [training/recovery],
> figured you'd have sharp opinions. Worth a 30-min call? → [calendly link]

**Re-engaging an existing connection / someone who liked a post:**
> Hey [name] — saw you [liked the biological-age post]. That's exactly what I'm building at Antiaging
> Labs. Taking on a few founding members this month; want me to send details or just find a time?

**Why this works:** the connection note earns the right to DM by referencing real content; the DM
opens on the universal frustration ("normal" labs) plus the wedge (wrong reference ranges) before
naming the product, and flatters with a *reason* ("you take recovery seriously") rather than empty
praise.

## X / Reddit / communities

Lowest trust, so direct DMs read as spam fastest. The play here is mostly **be useful in public
first** — answer biological-age / ApoB / VO2-max / South-Asian-health questions with genuine value,
mention what you're building only when relevant, and let people come to you. Reserve DMs for people
you've already had a real exchange with.

**Reply that earns a profile click (in a thread about bloodwork/biological age):**
> The reference-range problem is underrated here — most Indian labs flag against Western baselines,
> so South Asians get told they're "normal" at risk levels that aren't. (It's the whole reason I'm
> building [Antiaging Labs] around South-Asian-calibrated ranges + biological-age tracking.) Happy
> to share what markers actually matter if useful.

**DM only after a real exchange:**
> Enjoyed the back-and-forth on [thread]. I'm onboarding a small founding group for the
> biological-age thing I mentioned — calibrated for South Asian biology. Want me to send a quick
> overview? No pressure either way.

**Where to spend time:** Indian fitness/longevity subreddits and Discords, quantified-self and
biohacking spaces, longevity X. Communities have anti-promo norms — read the room, contribute 5x
more than you mention yourself, and never paste the same line twice in a thread.

## Follow-ups

Most consults come from the second or third touch, not the first — but follow-up is where founders
get spammy. One useful follow-up, then stop. Add value or a reason; never just "bumping this."

**Follow-up 1 (3-4 days later, add something):**
> Hey [name], no worries if the timing's off. In case it's useful regardless — here's a sample of
> the actual report so you can see what you'd get: [/client-5]. Door's open if you ever want to dig in.

**Follow-up 2 / graceful close (only if warranted):**
> All good [name], I'll leave it here. If "are my normal labs actually normal *for me*" ever starts
> nagging at you, you know where to find me. 🙂

**Why:** the first follow-up gives them something (the sample report does the selling), the second
closes the loop with dignity and a memorable hook, so the door stays open without nagging.

## Confirmations & no-show reduction

A booked consult is worthless if they ghost, and 30-50% will without a nudge — busy professionals
double-book and forget. A couple of short, warm, genuinely-helpful touches between booking and call
recover most of them. These are reminders, not re-pitches: keep them human and frictionless, and
always give an easy out (reschedule) so a no-show becomes a reschedule instead of a dead lead.

**On booking (immediate):**
> Locked in for [day/time], looking forward to it [name]. I'll keep it tight and useful — come with
> whatever's been bugging you about your health data and we'll dig in. If anything shifts, here's the
> link to grab another slot: [calendly link].

**Day before:**
> Hey [name] — still good for [time] tomorrow? If it's easier I can send a 2-min thing to skim
> beforehand so we make the most of the call. Either way, see you then 🙂

**Day-of (a few hours before):**
> Looking forward to [time] today, [name]. Here's the link: [meet/call link]. If something came up,
> no stress — reschedule here: [calendly link].

**Ghosted (no-show, same day or next):**
> No worries [name], stuff comes up. Want to grab another time? Here's my calendar: [calendly link].
> Or if the timing's just not right, totally fine to tell me and I'll get out of your inbox.

In the tracker, mark a confirmed call (`next_action` = "call [time]; confirmed") and use the
`no_show` status for ghosts so the founder knows to send the reschedule nudge rather than chase as
if they replied. Why this works: the reminders add a little value (the skim doc), assume the best,
and make rescheduling a one-tap yes — so the natural outcome of "I forgot" is a new slot, not a
lost lead.

## Content (credibility layer)

Content isn't the engine here — outreach is — but a steady drip makes every DM land softer and
pulls a little inbound. Post in the founder's real voice; build-in-public + genuinely useful
health insight both work for this audience. End with a soft consult CTA, not a hard sell.

**Build-in-public post angle:** what you're building and why ("got my bloodwork back, everything
'normal,' realized the ranges weren't even built for Indian bodies — so I'm building…"). Founders
who share the *why* get followers who become users.

**Educational post angle:** one genuinely useful idea — why biological age ≠ chronological age, why
ApoB matters more than the cholesterol line your doctor circled, why South Asian reference ranges
differ. Teach first; the credibility does the selling. Close with: "I'm building this into Antiaging
Labs and onboarding a small founding group — DM me or comment 'in' if you want a look."

**Format for LinkedIn:** strong first line (it's the only part shown before "see more"), short
lines, one idea, a light CTA. No hashtag soup. For X: a tight thread, one claim per post, the CTA
in the last post.

Keep posts honest by the same rules as DMs — teach and show, don't promise outcomes.

## Voice and formatting notes

- **Never shrink the product to "bloodwork + a wearable."** That framing makes it sound like every
  other tracker and throws away the whole differentiator. The wedge is the *integration*: genome /
  DNA + bloodwork + wearables (and DEXA/MRI where relevant) unified into one biological-age engine.
  Even a short message should signal that breadth — "your DNA, bloodwork and wearable in one read"
  beats "your bloodwork and Whoop." Lead with the relatable parts, but keep the genome in the frame.
- Write like a sharp founder texting a peer, not a marketing department. Contractions, plain words.
- Match the recipient's register. A doctor gets a slightly more clinical line; a CrossFit friend
  gets a looser one.
- One emoji max, and only where it'd feel natural. None in cold/professional DMs.
- Never paste two identical messages to two people who might compare notes. Vary the opener.
- Always surface the personalization hook as a separate note so the founder can correct it — you're
  often guessing at the detail, and a wrong detail is worse than none.
- Default CTA wording options, smallest-commitment first: "want me to send a 2-min overview?" →
  "want the details?" → "worth a quick 30-min call? [link]". Pick one.
