# Founding Cohort Outreach — Antiaging Labs

The job is concrete: **turn the right strangers into 10-15 founding members.** The pivot point is a
booked 30-minute consult at `https://calendly.com/company-kalman-labs/30min` — but a booked call is
the middle of the funnel, not the end. The full path is: *find a qualified person → get a reply →
book the consult → make sure they show up → convert them on the call → ask for a referral.*
Everything this skill produces — lists, DMs, posts, reminders, call prep, plans, the tracker — exists
to move one qualified person one step further down that path, without sounding like spam and without
saying anything the product can't back up. Optimizing only the top (more DMs) while leaking the
bottom (no-shows, calls that don't close) is the most common way this fails; the skill covers the
whole funnel for that reason.

Before producing anything, read **`references/product-brief.md`** once. It holds the current
positioning, offer, pricing, who-it's-for, what's actually true (and what is off-limits to
claim), and the consult-first logic. Outreach that drifts off that brief is worse than no
outreach, because it burns a real human relationship the founder can't get back.

## The current GTM context (as of this build)

These are the founder's locked-in choices. Treat them as defaults, not law — if the founder
says otherwise in the moment, follow them.

- **Conversion goal:** booked Calendly consults (not direct checkout). The bar is *qualified*
  consults, not vanity volume. Ten calls with real fits beat fifty with tyre-kickers.
- **Audience:** Indian metro professionals, roughly 28-50, high-earning and data-literate —
  founders, tech/finance operators, doctors, serious lifters/runners in Bangalore, Mumbai,
  Delhi, Hyderabad, Pune. People who already wear an Oura/Whoop/Apple Watch and have felt the
  limits of a normal "your reports are normal" checkup.
- **Channels:** warm personal network + WhatsApp, LinkedIn (posts + DMs), and
  X/Reddit/longevity communities.
- **Motion:** outreach-first (direct messages are the engine), with content as the credibility
  layer that makes the outreach land and creates a trickle of inbound.

## What to do when this skill triggers

Figure out which of these the founder needs right now, then go. Don't make them sit through a
plan when they asked for a single DM; don't hand them one DM when they asked for a week of work.

When in doubt, ask one sharp question rather than guessing wide. But if the founder has given
you a name, a screenshot of a profile, or a list, you usually have enough — get to a draft fast,
because their willingness to actually send things drops the longer they wait.

## Principles that make outreach work (and why)

**Personalize the first line or don't send it.** The single biggest predictor of a reply is
whether the opening proves you're talking to *them*, not blasting a list. Always anchor on a real
detail — their post, their company, a mutual contact, the marathon they just ran. If you don't
have a detail, your job is to go find one (ask the founder, or read what they've shared), not to
paper over it with "Hi, hope you're doing well."

**Lead with their problem, not your product.** The reader cares that their "normal" bloodwork
stopped feeling reassuring, that they're optimizing in the dark, that nobody reads their markers
against South Asian bodies. The engine, the twin, the clocks — those are how, and how comes
later, ideally on the call. Open on the itch, not the scratch.

**One ask, low friction.** Every message ends with the same small yes: a 30-minute consult, or
even just "want me to send the details?" Never stack two asks. Never attach a wall of links. The
goal of the message is a reply, not a sale.

**Short. Then shorter.** A cold-ish DM that runs past ~5-6 lines reads as a pitch and gets
archived. Warm WhatsApp can be looser. When you finish a draft, cut it 20%.

**Honesty is the brand.** The site deliberately avoids hype ("we don't make anti-aging claims,
we move markers and re-test"). Outreach must match. Use only the claims in the brief's
"safe to say" list. If a line would make a careful doctor wince, rewrite it. Overpromising to get
a consult poisons the call before it starts.

**These are real people the founder has to live with.** Founder-led outreach is a relationship,
not a campaign. No fake scarcity, no manipulative pressure, no mass-identical blasts that will
embarrass the founder when two friends compare notes. Quality and respect convert better here
anyway.

## Sending is the founder's job, not yours

Draft everything; **never send on the founder's behalf** unless they explicitly hand you a tool
and tell you to send a specific message. Messaging real people is irreversible and outward-facing.
The right output is copy the founder can paste, plus (when useful) the tracker row.

### Email drafts (the one real "automation")

For email outreach specifically, if a Gmail/email MCP is connected, you can remove the copy-paste
step by writing the message straight into the founder's **drafts** folder for them to review and
send. This is the appropriate envelope of automation for the first 10-15 users: it saves grunt work
while keeping the founder's hand on the send button (per the brief, cold mass-sending converts badly
here and risks their reputation anyway).

How to do it well:
- Use the connected Gmail tool's **create-draft** action (e.g. `create_draft` on whatever Gmail MCP
  is connected this session — don't hardcode a server id; pick the available email-draft tool).
- Create **drafts only. Never send.** Sending is the founder's explicit, per-message decision.
- One draft per recipient, each personalized — never a single blast to many `to:` addresses.
- Personalize the subject too (a generic subject kills open rates); keep the body to the same short,
  problem-first, one-CTA shape as the playbook templates.
- After creating drafts, tell the founder how many you made and where, and add/There-update the
  tracker rows (`status contacted` once they confirm they've sent, or a note that drafts are waiting).
- Don't invent email addresses. If you only have a name/LinkedIn, say so and keep it as paste-copy
  rather than guessing an address.

Note this is for **email**. WhatsApp, LinkedIn, X, and Reddit have no send-automation here (and
shouldn't — their terms and the relationship cost make hand-sending the right move); for those,
output paste-ready copy.

## Output shape

For a single message: give the channel, the recipient, and the message in a copy-paste block.
Add a one-line "why this opener" note so the founder can swap the personalization if yours is off.

For a batch: a short table (name · channel · angle) followed by each message in its own block, in
send-ready order. Offer to write them to the tracker.

For content: the post itself, ready to paste, plus a one-line note on what to pin/reply with when
people engage.

For a plan: the funnel math up top (so the numbers are honest about how much outreach 15 consults
takes), then a day-by-day list of concrete actions with quotas.

Keep your own commentary lean. The founder wants things they can use today, not an essay about
marketing.
